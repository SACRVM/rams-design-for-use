# What made Braun under Rams different

Rams was Braun's head of design from 1961 to 1995. Many companies made modern-looking appliances; Braun became a legend for products whose form came out of use, and for making that a company policy rather than a finish. The voices below are sourced; each section ends with what it means for software. Quotation marks mark exact wording, with the speaker named; everything else is paraphrase.

## Made for people
In a 1976 speech in New York, Rams put the skill's core idea in his own words: "You cannot understand good design if you do not understand people; design is made for people." Design used to impress or polish things up was, for him, no design at all: "This is packaging." He wanted products for "intelligent and responsible users – not consumers" ([Rams, "Design by Vitsœ", 1976](https://www.vitsoe.com/files/assets/1000/17/VITSOE_Dieter_Rams_speech.pdf)).

**For software:** the starting point is the people who will use it, never the effect it makes on whoever is building it.

## Design was company policy, not decoration
In 1954 Erwin Braun brought Fritz Eichler to the company to build a design department; it shaped the products and the company's whole image, and Eichler joined the board in 1960 ([kultur-frankfurt.de](https://kultur-frankfurt.de/portal/en/Design/FritzEichler/600/0/29264/mod1085-details1/1428.aspx)). The SK 4 radio-phonograph (1956) was a joint effort of Braun's management, the new design department and the Ulm School of Design (HfG Ulm), with Hans Gugelot giving it its shape ([museum-digital](https://bawue.museum-digital.de/object/71478)).

Eichler's ten theses on Braun design (1972/73, in the translation of designundtext.com) open with: "Braun designers are not guided by rigid rules — but by the goals and conditions that a task presents them with", and state: "Design is done for the people and has to consider the power, the size, the senses of human beings" ([Eichler, 10 theses](https://www.designundtext.com/en/1.11_3.8_eichler-10-theses-braun-design.php)).

Rams later said that good design normally needs "a strong relationship between an entrepreneur and the head of design". At Braun he reported directly to Erwin and Artur Braun while they ran the company, which ended when it passed to Gillette in 1967; he saw the same relationship "between Steve Jobs and Jony Ive" (Rams in The Telegraph, 2011, via [Edible Apple](https://www.edibleapple.com/2011/07/25/dieter-rams-on-apple-design-they-achieved-what-i-never-did/)). Ive, in turn, said of Rams: "He articulated the way it should be" ([Rams Foundation interview](https://rams-foundation.org/magazine/interviews/jonathan-ive/)).

**For software:** how something is used is decided by everyone who builds it, from the first decision on, with the body and senses of the people using it in mind, not painted on at the end. You and the person in front of the screen are that team.

## A signal only where it tells people something
Braun's palette was mostly greys, whites and black. By Braun's own account, Rams used colour only where it told the user something ([Braun](https://www.braun-audio.com/en-ES/stories/design/braun-colour-choices/)); the Rams Foundation writes that the coloured features on push-button switches "always have a clearly recognisable indicating function" ([Rams Foundation](https://rams-foundation.org/?p=7213)). On the ET 66 calculator (Rams and Dietrich Lubs, 1987) the eye is drawn to the yellow equals key, "the most frequently used function" ([V&A](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1360553)).

**For software:** emphasis is a signal, whether a colour, a sound or a vibration, and signals are scarce. Spend them on meaning and on what people use most, and keep everything else quiet so the signal can be noticed.

## There when needed, in the background otherwise
About his 606 shelving system for Vitsœ (not a Braun product, same designer, same thinking), Rams said it should be like a good English butler: "there when you need it but be in the background when you don't" ([Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/programs/dieters-top-ten/)).

**For software:** the interface serves; it does not perform. It is ready the moment it is needed and silent when it is not.

## Help people, don't dominate them
"And design shouldn't dominate people, it should help people," Rams said in a filmed interview for the publisher gestalten ([Rams Foundation](https://rams-foundation.org/?p=7213)).

**For software:** the person decides what happens and when. Nothing pushes, nags or decides for them.

## Built to last, and to be learned once
The 606 was designed in 1960 and has been "made by Vitsœ ever since"; a system bought decades ago can still be extended ([Vitsœ 606](https://www.vitsoe.com/gb/606)). The iPhone's original calculator app is widely seen as an homage to the Braun calculators of Rams and Lubs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieter_Rams)).

**For software:** what people have learned is worth keeping. A product that stays recognisable across years respects the effort its users put in.

## Not set in stone
Rams wrote that his principles are "not set in stone" and that each generation should review them and, if necessary, modify or supplement them ([letter to the Steve Jobs Archive, 2024](https://letters.stevejobsarchive.com/dieter-rams)).

**For software:** this skill is such a supplement. Apply the thinking, not the letter.
